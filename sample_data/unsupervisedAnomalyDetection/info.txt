1. The source is an SEM image of a microchip.
2. Advanced computer vision and AI methods are used to remove the noise and identify all the instances of the components of interest (a specific type of metal interconnects/logic routing). 
3 and 4. Each individual instance is given a unique index and is precisely located and traced for segmentation from the original image.
5. Each segmented instance is passed through our specialised deep-CNN trained on microchip components to extract a large number of textural and morphometric features. A large matrix of num_or_components by features is generated from the latent space.
6. While PCA reduces the dimensionality along the axes of maximum variances (global variations), the difference between an authentic and a trojan component may not necessarily be found on projections with large variances. PCA is therefore combined with an isolation forest anomaly score which measures variations from the high-dimensionality data.
7. The graph of IsolationForestAnomalyScore vs PC1 is plotted and gated (-30 to 30 for PC1 and 0.55 for IsolationForestAnomalyScore). Data points beyond either of these limits are considered suspects (orange) and data points beyond both of these limits are considered strong suspects (red).
8. The suspects and strong suspects data points (few out of a thousand) are then traced back to the location of the source components on the original image (orange and red traces around the respective components) via the index. These are then presented to the human experts for further consideration.
